# How this wins

## What actually decided the last two wins

Neither came from a novel idea. ParallaxPay won by being early to x402 when almost nobody
was building on it. Sage won on real payouts and growth metrics, in the founder's own words,
not on the product being cleverer than the room.

Stocklana offers no early advantage: everyone starts the same Friday, with the same issuers
and the same public repos. So the lever is not novelty. **It is real, verifiable usage, and
a product that still runs in November.**

## The two rooms

**Stocklana** closes 18 September, judged through 2 October, and asks one question: could
this be a real app people use. They look for a real use case, a working end-to-end path,
something that makes sense on Solana specifically, and execution quality.

**Crypto World's Fair** runs 14 September to 12 October, is cross-chain, and its judges are
investors. They look for an MVP, user acquisition, monetization and team. Winners can enter
an accelerator with $250K. Stocklana submissions are eligible, so this is one build.

## The appendix that wins both

Not a slide. A list of things that happened, each openable:

- payouts settled, and the value released
- distinct recipients, and how many were new books
- grams and share-equivalents outstanding
- receipts published, each a link
- **keep-rate at thirty days** — the share of released value still held
- one issuer conversation, started early enough to have a reply

Keep-rate is the number nobody else will have, and the one that cannot be faked. It requires
the cohort snapshot at release, which is why that is in the program and not in an analytics
afterthought.

## How the first people arrive

In order of strength, and none of them is a paid leaderboard:

1. **They were paid into it.** Somebody released value and it landed as ownership. The
   recipient never decided to invest.
2. **They were sponsored.** An issuer funded first grams into an empty book. The budget
   exists on their side already through holder-reward programmes.
3. **Someone gifted them a named slice.** An occasion, not a transfer.

Week one is seeded personally, the same way Sage's first campaigns were, to have something
real to point at. It converts to sponsor money after that, and the conversion is itself a
metric investors care about.

## Promotion

The founder runs the payer side in public and posts the receipts. A receipt is a better post
than a screenshot because anyone can open it. This is the loop that worked before and the
only one with evidence behind it.

## What would make this lose

- Building a trading terminal by accident, one feature at a time.
- Calling a fund share a gram, or any other claim a judge can check and disprove.
- Shipping a demo that has never had a stranger use it.
- Spending the month on an issuer switch we do not control.
- Letting the pitch require a second product to explain.
